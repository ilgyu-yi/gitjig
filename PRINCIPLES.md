# Principles

These principles govern this new product and its design. Their application to adopting repositories is expressed through product capabilities and contracts. Repository policy is configurable; the product's own agent-standing obligations are not waived by that configuration. Human actions outside product control are not certified as compliant product-managed completion.

## P1. Preserve an executable end-to-end workflow

- P1.1 Required stages have reachable entries, sufficient inputs, and usable continuations.
- P1.2 Mechanisms compose without contradicting or obstructing the prescribed normal path.
- P1.3 Work can resume from retained state and evidence after interruption.

## P2. Keep solutions proportionate to their purpose

- P2.1 Assess both the problem and proposed solution against Mission, Principles, Design, and Contracts, at depth proportionate to impact. This is one integrated judgment, not four mandatory ceremonies for every small edit. A mismatch requires reconsidering the solution or explicitly changing the authoritative decision; it cannot be silently ignored.
- P2.2 Prefer existing capabilities that provide the necessary guarantees. Additional precision or machinery must address an important failure a simpler alternative leaves open.
- P2.3 Distinguish required guarantees from conditions introduced by a proposed solution. The latter remain challengeable.
- P2.4 Include development, documentation, verification, review, operation, and future change in total cost.

## P3. Preserve agreed intent

- P3.1 Keep requirements, implementation, documentation, and verification consistent.
- P3.2 Change requirements or completion conditions through an explicit authorized decision.
- P3.3 Separate unmet requirements from new improvement proposals. Completion claims require evidence appropriate to their scope.

## P4. Require independent, effective verification

- P4.1 Changes receive review by a reviewer independent of the author. Review judgment and authority to accept or merge are separate responsibilities.
- P4.2 Independence concerns the production of judgment. Account separation is one form of evidence, not a universal definition. Same-account agent review does not constitute a separate-account platform approval.
- P4.3 Tests detect important violations of required behavior and invariants. Expectations come from requirements and contracts, and observations must reach the behavior claimed.
- P4.4 Preserve necessary verification while minimizing execution and maintenance costs. Test counts, copied implementation, and literal document preservation do not establish sufficiency.

## P5. Make delegated standing conditional

- P5.1 An agent obtains legitimate representative standing for a task only by satisfying the contractual rules and procedures for the relevant action.
- P5.2 Standing permits exercise of explicitly delegated authority held by the account owner. It creates no new account permissions.
- P5.3 Delegation defines a conditional ceiling, not immediate permission to act. Changed work or relevant conditions require renewed eligibility.
- P5.4 Normal merge and discretionary override use the owner's corresponding actual permissions. Neither successful review nor an agent-generated assertion creates authority.

## P6. Place responsibility and records at the appropriate level

- P6.1 Agent-level governs agent execution and review orchestration; local-level supports actor-neutral local development; repo-level supports repository rule configuration, developer guidance, and change acceptance.
- P6.2 Each level is independently useful. Agent-only procedures do not become mandatory human or repository rituals merely because the levels are integrated.
- P6.3 Keep detailed agent operating records with agent-level execution. Retain repository-facing intent, changes, verification, and acceptance rationale where repository participants can use them.
- P6.4 Repo-level provides adaptable recommendations, easy customization, and guidance consistent with applied rules.

## P7. Enforce required procedures at action boundaries

- P7.1 Required agent procedures gate the actions they govern; reminders and self-declared completion are insufficient.
- P7.2 Use execution-generated evidence linked to the reviewed work. Ordinary editing must not casually rewrite authoritative procedure state.
- P7.3 Explain refusal and recovery. Distinguish passed, failed, unavailable, and inactive enforcement.
- P7.4 State enforcement limits honestly. Optimize first for preventing shortcuts and accidental bypass, rather than defending against a malicious operator.

## P8. Counter agent bias and make review converge

This principle's procedural obligations apply to agent-driven work.

- P8.1 Independently challenge a plan's premises and materially different approaches before implementation. Evaluate findings by evidence rather than role authority.
- P8.2 Seek counterexamples to completion claims. Neither the planner nor challenger monopolizes both alternatives and adjudication.
- P8.3 Account for every received finding before claiming resolution. Preserve pending obligations across review replacement and recovery; a newer clear result cannot erase them. Resolve valid findings, revalidate affected work, and separate exact mechanical remedies from substantive changes.
- P8.4 Separate user communication language from the English working representation. Normalize intent without silently altering it.
- P8.5 Handle repeated findings and judgment conflicts explicitly. Do not invent new requirements or perpetuate review after agreed obligations are satisfied.
